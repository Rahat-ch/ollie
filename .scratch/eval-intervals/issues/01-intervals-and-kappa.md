# 01: Intervals and kappa in the eval report

**What to build:** Every rate in the eval report (Evidence Integrity, detection share, false-positive rate, Story and Summary first-attempt and valid rates, the Judge's agreement) carries a Wilson 95 percent interval beside it in the JSON, on the same line in the text report, and as a bracket or range mark on the charts, produced by one function at the stats module's chokepoint so no rate can be printed without one; a total of zero is stated as undefined rather than a false precision. The Judge's calibration reports Cohen's kappa against the human verdicts, the two-by-two confusion matrix, and the agreement an always-pass and an always-fail Judge would score on the same set; the gate becomes agreement at or above the existing threshold and kappa at or above a named floor of 0.6, and the withheld message names which condition failed. The report keeps every existing field and adds the new ones beside them. The evals README explains the interval and kappa in a paragraph with an example each, and the write-up's eval section quotes the small-sample rates as ranges from the committed report. The fake run exercises all of it.

## Comments

**2026-09-17, implementation.** `wilsonInterval(hits, total)` in `src/evals/stats.ts` is the one place a count becomes an interval: the Wilson score interval at 95 percent with `Z_95 = 1.96`, `null` when the total is zero, and the two bounds pinned to exactly 0 and 1 at the ends of the scale, where the arithmetic otherwise returns 0.9999999999999999 — an early version of the report-wide test caught that on a Learner whose Wilson upper missed 1 by a float. It sits beside `share` and `integrityRate`, and every rate the report carries is computed with it at the same line as the rate itself: `writtenValidity` gains `firstAttemptRateInterval` and `validRateInterval`; `HypothesisSplit` gains `detectionRateInterval`, `falsePositiveRateInterval`, and `evidenceIntegrityInterval`; `LearnerHypotheses` gains `falsePositiveRateInterval` and `evidence.integrityInterval`; `StoryReadability` and `SummaryFaithfulness` gain `passRateInterval`; `Calibration` gains `agreementInterval`. The convention is `<rate>Interval` beside `<rate>`, every existing field untouched, so earlier reports and every existing reader still work. Nothing that is not a proportion got one: the convergence means are means, not shares of a denominator.

`calibrateJudge` in `src/evals/judge.ts` now builds the two-by-two `Confusion` of the Judge's verdicts against the human labels (`humanPassJudgePass` and the other three), Cohen's kappa from it (zero when chance already explains everything, which is exactly what an always-pass Judge scores), `alwaysPassAgreement` and `alwaysFailAgreement` — the set's pass share and fail share, the agreement the two trivial Judges get for free — and `JUDGE_KAPPA_FLOOR = 0.6` beside `JUDGE_AGREEMENT_THRESHOLD = 0.8`. `passes` is now both conditions, and `withheld` is the failed ones in words: "agreement 0.60 is below the threshold 0.80, and kappa 0.00 is below the floor 0.60". The Calibration Sets, the rubrics, the Learners, the Coach, and what any eval measures are untouched; this ticket is arithmetic and presentation.

The text report (`src/evals/format.ts`) prints `95% CI 0.058 to 0.392` on the same line as the rate it qualifies, to three places because at the ends two would round the width away — `ci` and `rate` are the two new helpers. The Judge now reads over three lines: what it agreed on with its interval, then "kappa 0.00, floor 0.60 (an always-pass Judge would score 60%, an always-fail Judge 40%)", then "Readability: scores withheld — …" naming the conditions. On the charts, `bracket` and `rangeLine` are new marks in `src/evals/chart-kit.ts` (`data-mark="interval"`, a thin rule with a tick at each bound): the two validity charts bracket each of their three bars and the calibration meter — on the Story chart the agreement interval's upper bound stops just short of the threshold line, which is the point of the whole ticket in one picture — and carry the kappa line and the withheld reason in the Judge's panel; the Evidence Integrity chart gets a 0-to-1 range line under its headline; detection, false positives, and plan sources print the interval in their summary lines. The charts compute each interval from the counts the report holds, so a report written before this ticket still draws (checked against `2026-09-13T17-38-41Z.json`, which renders all seven charts and falls back to the old withheld sentence it has no `withheld` for).

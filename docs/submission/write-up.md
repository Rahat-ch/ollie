# Ollie — a Grade 1 math game that learns how you learn

**Most math apps adapt difficulty. Ollie adapts to how the learner is learning.**

Live at [ollie.rahatcodes.com](https://ollie.rahatcodes.com) · source at [github.com/Rahat-ch/ollie](https://github.com/Rahat-ch/ollie)

## What it does

Ollie is a tablet web game for a six-year-old who cannot yet read the screen. Ollie the owl reads every Problem aloud and the Learner answers by tapping a number; there is no typing anywhere in her flow. A Session is 6 to 10 Problems with a ten-frame or a number line beside them, a Repeat button on every one, a hand-written Hint after the first miss, and a Reveal with no penalty after the second. No timers, no lives, no leaderboards.

After every Session an AI Coach reads the Session Log, writes what it now believes about the Learner as Hypotheses — each carrying its evidence as the actual Problem IDs it rests on — and plans the next Session inside a space the engine bounds. The Parent holds a button for three seconds to reach the Parent Area and read all of it: a Parent Summary per Session with its evidence separated into first-try correct, Hint-assisted, and Revealed; Mastery per Skill; and Ollie's Notebook, where every belief opens to show the Problems behind it. Mastery changes the game rather than the wallet: Mastering a strategy teaches Ollie a **Power** — Count-On Flight, Make-Ten Magic, Missing Number Detective, Story Solver — which Ollie then visibly uses on every matching Problem. Coins, a six-item Shop, and a Streak with two Freezes reward turning up, never accuracy.

The content is **a focused Grade 1 arithmetic progression aligned to key CCSS 1.OA and 1.NBT concepts**. It is not the whole of Grade 1, and the README's table maps each of the seven Skills to its standard. The pedagogy, the choice of strategies, and the decision to drop voice input rest on the reading in [`docs/research/k5-math-game/`](../research/k5-math-game/).

## What is generative and what is not

Generative: the **Stories** (the two-sentence word problems written in the Learner's Theme around the engine's numbers, with a placeholder the device fills with her Nickname), the **Coach's Hypotheses and Session Plans**, the **words of the Parent Summary**, and **Ollie's voice audio**.

Deterministic: the **arithmetic**, the **answers**, the **Hints**, **Plan validation**, the **evidence checks**, **Mastery**, **Powers**, **Coins**, and the **Streak**.

Nothing in the second list is ever produced or altered by a model, and every item in the first list passes a deterministic check before a child or a parent sees it.

## How it was built

![Session, engine, evidence, Coach, validator, next Session: the AI can personalise the learning path, it cannot make up the math](./architecture.svg)

**The AI can personalise the learning path. It cannot make up the math.** Two seams carry the whole app.

**The Loop** is a pure function: given a Session Plan, the Profile, a seed, and an answer policy it returns the Session Log, the updated Knowledge Estimates, the Powers earned, and the next Profile. The seven Skills are template families that compute their own answers; Bayesian Knowledge Tracing updates from the **first attempt only** (a hinted success is evidence for the Coach, never a second mastery observation); Mastered is an Estimate of 0.95 with 8 of the last 10 first attempts correct; response time is context that can never lower Mastery. Having no I/O, the same function runs the browser, the CLIs, the Baseline, and the simulation.

**Generation** is one interface with four operations — write Story, run Coach, write Parent Summary, render speech — and a fake that every test runs on. The real adapters are Claude Opus 5 (Coach, Parent Summary, eval Judge) and Sonnet 5 (Stories) through the Anthropic SDK with structured output, and ElevenLabs for the voice.

Between them sits the part I care most about. The Coach is handed the Session's evidence, the Learner Notes, the Estimates, and the **Plan Space** — Skills, per-Skill number ranges inside the standard, structures, review share, length 6 to 10, and the Hypothesis under test. Nothing outside it can be planned, so the Coach cannot skip a prerequisite or leave the grade. The engine then checks what comes back: a Hypothesis citing a Problem the Coach was never shown is rejected, and so is any Plan outside the space. One retry carries every reason back; a second failure falls through to the Baseline Plan, and Ollie's Notebook tells the Parent that it did. Neither the Coach nor the Parent Summary is ever given the Nickname.

Stories are written with a placeholder where the Nickname goes, which the device fills in, and validated deterministically — exactly the engine's digits, two sentences, under 25 words, only that Theme's closed word list — then kept in a **Content Pool** keyed by Theme, Skill, structure, and numbers, so most Sessions need no call; a miss is written live and added, and a template sentence stands in if that fails. The **Speech Chain** tries the line's audio, the bundled fixed line, the platform's speech synthesis, then the line on screen, so no Session is ever silent and blank. The Profile lives in localStorage: no accounts, no database, no child data. The Nickname is the one personal word that leaves the device, and it leaves only so that a line addressed to the Learner can be spoken aloud — the speech route and nothing else, which is what onboarding tells the Parent before play begins (ADR 0002).

The loop is evaluated against a **Baseline** — the same Loop with the Coach replaced by a fixed 8-of-10 gate and a fixed 6+2 composition — over six seeded **Simulated Learners** with planted weaknesses, two held out of prompt tuning. `pnpm eval` runs 240 Sessions, scores detection, false positives, Evidence Integrity, Story and Summary validity, and a calibrated Judge, and writes a dated JSON report and the chart.

## What the evals say

The eval has now run live, three times, back to back on 2026-09-18: [`docs/evals/2026-09-18T18-12-41Z.json`](../evals/2026-09-18T18-12-41Z.json), [`docs/evals/2026-09-18T18-41-44Z.json`](../evals/2026-09-18T18-41-44Z.json), and [`docs/evals/2026-09-18T19-09-56Z.json`](../evals/2026-09-18T19-09-56Z.json) — the real Opus 5 Coach, Sonnet 5 Story writer, Opus 5 Parent Summary writer, and Opus 5 Judge, 240 Sessions a run. The charts are drawn from the third: [convergence](../evals/convergence.svg), [evidence integrity](../evals/evidence-integrity.svg), [detection](../evals/detection.svg), [false positives](../evals/false-positives.svg), [plan sources](../evals/plan-sources.svg), [Story validity](../evals/story-validity.svg), and [Summary validity](../evals/summary-validity.svg).

The three runs share their seeds, their Learners, their Baseline, and every threshold — the Baseline column is identical in all three reports, byte for byte — so the only thing that moves between them is what the Coach wrote. **Every headline below is therefore a range, the lowest of the three runs to the highest**, and the range is the result; one run's figure is one draw. Where a single run's line is quoted it carries the 95% Wilson interval the report prints beside it, because these samples are small and a bare number would claim a precision they cannot support.

- **Evidence Integrity: 100 percent in every run and every split**, as the report prints it, over 31,711, 32,385 and 31,826 citations — 95,922 in all. Every Problem ID cited by every Notes written, rejected attempts included, is a Problem the engine actually showed the Coach, with one exception in 95,922: a single citation in run 2's fast-fatigue Learner (10,284 of 10,285 in that split, 99.99%, CI **0.999 to 1.000**), and it sits in an attempt the engine rejected, so it never reached Notes anyone can read. Beside it, **claim agreement 92 to 95 percent** (tuning 92%, 93%, 94%; held out 93%, 94%, 95% — run 1's tuning split is 92%, CI **0.920 to 0.927**). The two are different failures kept apart: Evidence Integrity is fabrication, whether the Problem cited exists at all; claim agreement is reading, whether the Assistance State of the Problems a claim cites says what the claim says — a difficulty claim citing Problems that took a Hint, a strength claim citing first-try correct ones, a contrastive claim ("first try when the smaller addend comes first, but a Hint when the larger does") citing one of each.
- **Detection: crossing ten was named in 2 of the 3 runs, after Session 6 in both; change unknown in 2 of 3, after Session 13 and after Session 16.** One weakness is planted per split, so a hit is 1 of 1 (100%, CI **0.207 to 1.000**) and a miss 0 of 1 (0%, CI **0.000 to 0.793**): four hits from six chances, and no sample that could tell a good detector from a lucky one. The run that missed crossing ten is the one worth reading. At Session 18 that Coach reported, supported, that make-a-ten "draws assistance most often, at roughly half its items, and the misses do not sort by structure, by which addend is larger, or by doubles" — which is the planted rate exactly, found at the Skill level: the Learner's make-a-ten ability of 0.85 less the 0.35 weakness penalty is 0.50 on a crossing item, and within 20 every make-a-ten item crosses ten. The scorer does not count that as naming the pattern, by design: a claim about a Skill is a claim about the curriculum, not a pattern in what the child does across Skills, and the whole point of the metric is to reward the second. What the Notes show is the Coach chasing sub-patterns inside the weak Skill and refuting them one by one — smaller-first against larger-first, doubles, the size of the whole — until what is left is the Skill and its rate, which is true, honestly arrived at, and scores zero.
- **False positives** (supported Hypotheses naming a weakness the Learner has not got): **4 of 39, 7 of 44, and 10 of 45** on the tuning split (10% to 22%; run 3's 10 of 45 is 22%, CI **0.125 to 0.363**), **3 of 30, 0 of 20, and 2 of 20** held out (0% to 10%). Nothing changed between those runs but the sampling: that is the width of a three-run spread on a forty-item sample, not a trend.
- **358 of 360 Plans accepted from the Coach on the first attempt, 2 after one retry, 0 Baseline fallbacks** across the three runs (120, 118, 120 first time). Not one Session of 360 fell through to the Baseline Plan.
- **Sessions to Mastery.** Mean Skills Mastered by Session 20, Coach against Baseline: **5.75, 5.75 and 6.50 against 6.00** on the four tuning Learners, **5.50, 5.50 and 5.00 against 6.00** on the two held out. Said plainly: **the Coach is not faster to Mastery than the fixed gate**, and on the held-out pair it is behind it in all three runs. The reason is in the design of both. The Coach spends Sessions testing a Hypothesis across Skills — three make-a-ten items and two unknown-addend ones to separate two explanations — while the Baseline drills one Skill until 8 of the last 10 first attempts are right and then moves to the next. Against a Simulated Learner whose ability is a fixed number, drilling wins by construction: these children cannot learn from practice, so a Session spent diagnosing is simply a Session not spent at the gate. That is a limit of the instrument as much as of the Coach, and a **practice effect in the Simulated Learners is the named next step**; until it exists, Sessions to Mastery measures how fast a planner confirms what is already true, not how fast a child learns.
- **Stories: 87 to 93 percent valid on the model's first attempt** (26, 27 and 28 of 30; run 1's 87% carries CI **0.703 to 0.947**), **97 to 100 percent valid within the three bounded attempts**, and **at most one template fallback** in any run (0, 1, 1). The rejections were Theme vocabulary and, three times over three runs, altered numbers — caught by the validator every time. **Readability 66 to 72 percent** of the valid Stories (19 of 29, 20 of 30, 21 of 29; run 3's 66% carries CI **0.473 to 0.801**), under a Judge that **agreed with the hand-labelled Calibration Set on 20 of 20 Stories in every run** (100%, CI **0.839 to 1.000**) with **kappa 1.00** against the 0.60 floor: the gate opened on its own terms rather than being waived. The Judge and the writers are both Claude models; that same-family limitation stands.
- **Summaries: 6 of 6 valid on the first attempt in every run** (100%, CI **0.610 to 1.000**), no template fallbacks, and **faithfulness 6 of 6 in every run**. The Judge agreed with the Calibration Set on **9 of 10 Summaries in run 1** (90%, CI **0.596 to 0.982**, kappa 0.80 — it failed one Summary the label passes) and **10 of 10 in runs 2 and 3** (100%, CI **0.722 to 1.000**, kappa 1.00), clearing the 0.80 threshold and the 0.60 kappa floor in all three, so the scores were reported rather than withheld.
- **Cost, measured.** **$26.27 to $27.12 a run** — $27.1220, $26.4639 and $26.2714, **$79.86 for the three** — of which the Coach is about 95 percent, at **$0.2083 to $0.2148 per Session**, so about **$0.21 a Session** averaged over the 20 Sessions across which its Notes grow. Every call behind the Generation seam reports its model, tokens and wall time to one recorder, and each report carries the telemetry per operation, per model and for the run. The dollars are the published rates applied to the measured tokens, not the invoice.

## What has and has not run live

All of it has. The vendors have been exercised with real keys since 2026-09-15: the Coach and the Parent Summary have run after real Sessions, the Story writer wrote Stories live and filled the Content Pool (2,790 keys, 5,520 Stories, every one passed by the validator), the voice was designed in ElevenLabs Voice Design, and every fixed line and question line is rendered and bundled. The last thing needing a key was `pnpm eval` with the real Coach and Judge, and on 2026-09-18 it ran three times; the numbers above are those runs, and every committed chart is drawn from the third. **Nothing in this entry is now waiting on a key.**

What the numbers are is a narrower thing than what they measure. Six hand-designed Simulated Learners, whose ability is a fixed number and who therefore cannot learn from practice; one planted weakness per split; and three runs. The gates, the validators, and Evidence Integrity are measured over tens of thousands of items and are tight. Everything that rests on the Coach's judgement — detection, false positives, Sessions to Mastery — rests on a handful, which is why it is quoted as a range across three runs with its intervals and not as a score.

## Next steps

How every claim above can be checked, test by test and with a live Coach trace, is in [`evidence.md`](./evidence.md).

Bring the cost per Session down for a paid product: the Coach runs on Opus 5 at high effort today, measured at $0.21 a Session across the three live runs; Sonnet 5 at medium effort with prompt caching should land near $0.05, and the eval is the instrument that says whether the cheaper Coach still finds the planted weaknesses — with tokens and cost per Session now measured in every report, that comparison is read off the two reports rather than estimated by hand. Then: a practice effect in the Simulated Learners, so Sessions to Mastery measures learning and not only confirmation; the rest of Grade 1 (compare problems, three addends, place value); a judge outside the Claude family; and a Coach that reads across Sessions rather than one at a time.

**Ollie learns how you learn.**

# Eval Runs

Every `pnpm eval` writes one dated JSON report here, named by its UTC time (`2026-09-11T23-05-00Z.json`), and regenerates the seven charts from that file. `pnpm eval:chart` redraws them from the latest report, or from any earlier one with `--report`. Reports are committed so a number quoted in the project README or below can be traced to the run that produced it. Each report says which Generation ran the Coach (`coach.generation`: `fake`, or the model id); the chart says so in its subtitle.

The charts (ticket 01 of the visuals) are drawn by code from a report file and never from a live run, in the app's paper and ink tokens, each with a title, a subtitle naming the Coach that ran and the report's date, and no number that is not in the report:

- `convergence.svg` — Skills Mastered by Session, Coach against Baseline, one facet per Simulated Learner.
- `evidence-integrity.svg` — Evidence Integrity as the headline, so 1.00 reads as "every citation real", with claim agreement beneath it and the three counts both are made of: the citations checked, the unknown Problem IDs, and the citations that disagree with their own claim.
- `detection.svg` — each Learner with a planted weakness on a Session timeline, marked at the Session a supported Hypothesis first named it, or called never.
- `false-positives.svg` — per Learner, the supported Hypotheses with the false ones marked off the end, tuning and held out labelled.
- `plan-sources.svg` — Coach, Coach after a retry, and Baseline fallback over every Session the Coach planned.
- `story-validity.svg` and `summary-validity.svg` — first attempt, within the bounded attempts, and template fallbacks, each bar with its interval bracketed over it, then the Judge's gate: the score reported, or withheld with the agreement fraction against the Calibration Set, its interval, kappa, and the threshold.

**What the interval and the kappa mean** (from ticket 01 of the intervals). Every rate in the report carries a 95% Wilson interval beside it: in the JSON as a sibling field (`falsePositiveRate` and `falsePositiveRateInterval`), on the same line in the text report, and on the charts as a bracket over the bar or a range line beside a headline number. The interval is the range of true rates the sample is consistent with, so a rate over a handful of items is never read as precise: 3 false positives of 18 supported Hypotheses is 17%, but the sample supports anywhere from 0.058 to 0.392; 30 valid Stories of 30 is not an exact 1.00 but 0.886 to 1.000. It is the Wilson score interval (z = 1.96, `wilsonInterval` in `src/evals/stats.ts`, the one place a rate is turned into an interval) rather than the textbook normal approximation, because the samples here are small and the rates sit at the ends of the scale, where the approximation claims a width of zero. A rate over nothing has no interval: the field is null and the report says undefined. Beside the Judge's raw agreement, its calibration carries Cohen's kappa, the two-by-two confusion matrix of its verdicts against the human labels, and the agreement the two trivial Judges would score — one that passes everything, one that fails everything. Kappa is the agreement left once each rater's own habits are taken out of it: the Story Calibration Set is 12 pass and 8 fail, so a Judge that says pass to everything agrees on 12 of 20 — 0.60, three quarters of the way to the gate — for a kappa of 0.00, and a Judge agreeing on 16 of 20 with that same 60/40 habit scores kappa 0.58. The gate is therefore both conditions, agreement at or above `JUDGE_AGREEMENT_THRESHOLD` (0.80) **and** kappa at or above `JUDGE_KAPPA_FLOOR` (0.60, `src/evals/judge.ts`), and a withheld verdict names the condition that failed in `judge.calibration.withheld`.

The run plays the six Simulated Learners (`src/evals/learners.ts`) for 20 Sessions each under the Coach and under the Baseline on identical seeds. The convergence section records, per Learner and per planner, the Session in which each Skill became Mastered, the first-try rate, and the share of Problems in the target accuracy band (a true first-try chance of 0.7 to 0.9). The hypotheses section scores the Coach run: for each planted weakness, the Session after which a supported Hypothesis first named it in its own words (crossing ten, missing addend), never just the Skill; false positives, supported Hypotheses naming a weakness the Learner does not have; **naming is gated on what the claim is about**: a supported Hypothesis names a weakness only when it says the pattern **and** reads as a difficulty or a contrast, so a strength or a claim with no polarity names nothing however many Skills it mentions ("Change-unknown holds when the change itself is large" is the Coach saying the child can do it, not a weakness), and the same gate decides detection and false positives. The Coach's own words for a crossing are matched with up to two words in the way ("the smallest crossing of ten", "crossing back over ten"), and the Skill name alone still never counts. Each Learner also carries `notesBySession`, the Notes the engine kept after every Session, so `pnpm eval:rescore --report <file>` can score a written report again with the current scorer and print the new detection and false positives beside the stored ones, without rewriting the file; **two numbers over every citation in every Notes the Coach wrote**, rejected attempts included, so an invented Problem ID counts even though the engine refused it; and how many Plans came from the Coach, from a retry, or from the Baseline fallback.

The two citation numbers are kept apart because they are different failures (from ticket 01 of the scorers). **Evidence Integrity** (`evidenceIntegrity`, `evidence.integrity`) is the fabrication metric: the citations whose Problem ID exists in the Log the Coach was shown, over every citation. It is the number that must be 1.00, and a Coach that invents a Problem is the only way to lose it. **Claim agreement** (`claimAgreement`, beside it in the JSON with its own interval) is scored over the citations that exist alone: the share whose Assistance State says what the claim says. A difficulty claim ("misses", "needs a Hint") cites Problems that were not first-try correct; a strength claim ("solid", "no Hints") ones that were, with a negated word counted as the other side ("no Hints" is a strength, "not yet secure" a difficulty); a refuted Hypothesis or a claim with no polarity is checked for the ID only. A **contrastive** claim — one that says both things ("not yet reliable: after a first-try correct compose item, this one ended in a Reveal") or sets one against the other with a but, while, whereas, except, or although — agrees when its cited set holds an outcome of each kind, which is exactly what such a claim asserts; when its citations show one kind only, it is read one-sided again and checked that way. Before the split, a live Coach's best Hypotheses — the contrastive ones — scored 45 to 51 percent with no invented ID at all, which is the fabrication number the metric was built to report saying nothing about fabrication. The two held-out Learners, change-unknown weakness and fast fatigue, are never used to tune a prompt; every report flags them and scores the tuning and held-out splits separately. Simulated Learners only; never a real child's data.

The stories section (from ticket 10) writes one Story per Theme and Unit 3 structure, 30 in all, for a seeded sample of Problems, with the Nickname placeholder. Validity is deterministic: the share accepted by the validator on the model's first attempt, the share that ended as a model Story within the three bounded attempts, how many fell back to the template sentence, and every rejection reason with its count (numbers altered or missing, too many words, a word outside the Theme's vocabulary, and so on). Readability is the Judge's: Claude Sonnet 5.5 (`claude-sonnet-5-5`, Opus 5 in the three live runs of 2026-09-18) with a written rubric (a six-year-old could follow it after one hearing; what happens matches the arithmetic; it reads as a scene in the Theme) over the valid Stories, but the score appears only when the Judge first agrees with the hand-labelled Calibration Set of 20 Stories (`src/evals/calibration.ts`, 12 labelled pass and 8 fail, all of which the validator accepts) on at least 80%; otherwise `judge.readability` is null and the text report says the scores were withheld. Every Story written, its rejections, and its judgement are kept in the report. On the fake, the Story writer is the template's second shape (always valid) and the Judge is the validator's opinion, which agrees with 12 of 20 labels and so fails calibration by design: a fake report shows the gate working, not the models. The Judge and the Story writer are both Claude models; that same-family limitation is stated in the results below.

The summaries section (from ticket 12) writes the Parent Summary the app would have written after each Simulated Learner's last Coach Session, six in all, from the engine's tally of that Session Log and the Notes the Coach left. Validity is deterministic: the share the Summary validator accepts on the model's first attempt and within the two bounded attempts, how many fell back to the hand-written template, and every rejection reason with its count (a number the Session Log does not support, a claim about how the Learner was thinking, a part too long or empty). A number the Log supports is one the engine's tally gave the writer or a sum of that tally (a Skill's own total, a state's total across the Skills practiced), a number inside a Skill or Power name, or a number in the words of a Hypothesis the Summary may say Ollie is watching; a Hypothesis's confidence and the Problem IDs it cites are still not numbers a Parent may read as evidence. Faithfulness is the Judge's: Opus 5 with a second written rubric (every claim supported by the evidence the Summary was written from; no claim about the Learner's thinking; first-try correct, Hint-assisted, and Revealed kept apart), but the score appears only when the Judge first agrees with the hand-labelled Calibration Set of 10 Summaries (`src/evals/calibration.ts`, 6 labelled pass and 4 fail, all of which the validator accepts) on at least 80%; otherwise `judge.faithfulness` is null and the text report says the scores were withheld. On the fake, the Summary writer is the tally in plain sentences (always valid) and the Judge is the validator's opinion, which agrees with 6 of 10 verdicts and so fails calibration by design.

The telemetry section (from the cost ticket) is what the run itself cost. Every call behind the Generation seam and every Judge call reports its model, its tokens — input, output, cache reads and cache writes kept apart — and its wall time to one recorder the eval command installs for the run (`src/generation/telemetry.ts`); the app's routes install none and record nothing. The report adds them up three ways: `telemetry.byOperation` per operation (`coach`, `summary`, `story`, `judge`, each present even when it never ran), `telemetry.byModel` per model, and `telemetry.total` for the run, each with the calls, the tokens, the milliseconds, the estimated dollars, and every model id the group named — so no report says only "the Coach" about which model ran. `telemetry.perSession` carries the Coach's own cost: dollars per Coach call and dollars per Session, which is the number a paid product is planned from. Since the timeout ticket (harness 03) the section also keeps each call as it was recorded, in `telemetry.calls`, and `telemetry.latency` gives each operation's p50 and p95 wall time per call by nearest rank (`null` for an operation that never ran), because a total or a mean cannot say how many Coach calls ran past a deadline; reports before it carry neither. The text report prints the same thing as a Cost block at the end, with a p50 and a p95 column. **The dollars are an estimate, not the invoice**: the tokens the models reported multiplied by the published first-party rates as they stood on the day the table was written (Sonnet 5.5 $2.00 in / $10.00 out per million tokens, added 2026-09-28; Opus 5 $5.00 / $25.00, Sonnet 5 $2.00 / $10.00, Haiku 4.5 $1.00 / $5.00; a cache read at a tenth of the input rate and a cache write at 1.25 times it). A model with no rate in that table is reported as `null` and printed as undefined rather than guessed at. Since harness ticket 06 every call runs on Sonnet 5.5 with the server-side refusal fallback (`fallbacks: "default"`), so a call Sonnet 5.5 declines can be served by its fallback model inside the same call: the call is then recorded as the model that served it, read from the response's `usage.iterations`, with each declined attempt kept beside it in `declined` and priced at its own model's rate. A decline before any output is counted in full, so the estimate errs high; `telemetry.byModel` adds each attempt to the model it ran on. On a fake run every call is reported with no tokens and no milliseconds through the same recorder, so the section is present and costs $0.0000 throughout: the fake run exercises the path, it does not price it.

**The three live runs.** The current reports are `2026-09-18T18-12-41Z.json` (run 1), `2026-09-18T18-41-44Z.json` (run 2) and `2026-09-18T19-09-56Z.json` (run 3): three `pnpm eval` runs of the real Opus 5 Coach, Sonnet 5 Story writer, Opus 5 Parent Summary writer and Opus 5 Judge, back to back on 2026-09-18, and the first reports here that are not from `--fake`. Every chart is drawn from run 3, the latest. Only the text output of each run was discarded; it is printed from the report file, so `pnpm eval:chart --report docs/evals/<date>.json` and a re-read of the JSON reproduce it.

Three runs on the same seeds is the smallest honest measure of the Coach's own variability. The Learners, their seeds, the Problems, the Baseline and every threshold are identical across the three: the Baseline column is the same in all three reports, byte for byte. The only thing that moves is what the Coach writes, so the spread between run 1 and run 3 on any Coach-scored number — detection, false positives, Sessions to Mastery, claim agreement — is the model's sampling and nothing else, and a number quoted from one run alone is quoting one draw. Every Coach-scored number in the results below is therefore given as a range across the three, never as a point. The spread is wide where the sample is small: false positives on the tuning split run 4 of 39, 7 of 44 and 10 of 45, and each of the two planted weaknesses is named in two of the three runs. It is narrow where the sample is large: Evidence Integrity and claim agreement are over tens of thousands of citations and move by three points at most. Evidence Integrity prints 100 percent in all three, with exactly one invented Problem ID in 95,922 citations — `fast-fatigue` in run 2, `evidence.unknownIds` 1 of 4,437, which is why that split's interval starts at 0.999 rather than 1.000 — and it is in an attempt the engine rejected, the retry counted in that Learner's `sources`.

Each run's cost is in its own `telemetry` section and is the measured tokens at the published rates, not an invoice: run 1 $27.1220 (227 calls, 2,098,012 tokens, $0.2148 per Coach Session), run 2 $26.4639 (227 calls, 2,072,878 tokens, $0.2091 per Session), run 3 $26.2714 (224 calls, 2,032,413 tokens, $0.2083 per Session) — $79.86 for the three, about 28 minutes of wall time each, the Coach being 95 percent of it.

The last fake report, and the one the charts were drawn from before these, is `2026-09-18T17-41-33Z.json`: it is the first report to carry the telemetry section. The chart tests read a fixed fake report of their own, `2026-09-18T00-15-08Z.json`, which stays where it is. `2026-09-18T14-19-52Z.json` before it is the first to carry each Learner's `notesBySession`. `2026-09-18T02-12-15Z.json` before that is the first to carry claim agreement beside Evidence Integrity. `2026-09-18T00-15-08Z.json` before it is the first with the intervals and the Judge's kappa (0.00 on both Calibration Sets, which withholds the fake Judge's scores twice over). Reports written before each of those carry neither field; the charts still draw them, computing every rate and interval from the counts the report holds.

The first report, `2026-09-11T00-16-16Z.json`, is from ticket 05 and has the Baseline alone; the chart refuses it with a message, and its numbers are reproduced byte for byte in every later report's `convergence.baseline`.

## What the three live runs say

The three runs share their seeds, their Learners, their Baseline, and every threshold, so the only thing that moves between them is what the Coach wrote. **Every headline below is therefore a range, the lowest of the three runs to the highest**, and the range is the result; one run's figure is one draw. Where a single run's line is quoted it carries the 95% Wilson interval the report prints beside it, because these samples are small and a bare number would claim a precision they cannot support. The charts are drawn from run 3: [convergence](./convergence.svg), [evidence integrity](./evidence-integrity.svg), [detection](./detection.svg), [false positives](./false-positives.svg), [plan sources](./plan-sources.svg), [Story validity](./story-validity.svg), and [Summary validity](./summary-validity.svg).

- **Evidence Integrity: 100 percent in every run and every split**, as the report prints it, over 31,711, 32,385 and 31,826 citations — 95,922 in all. Every Problem ID cited by every Notes written, rejected attempts included, is a Problem the engine actually showed the Coach, with one exception in 95,922: a single citation in run 2's fast-fatigue Learner (10,284 of 10,285 in that split, 99.99%, CI **0.999 to 1.000**), and it sits in an attempt the engine rejected, so it never reached Notes anyone can read. Beside it, **claim agreement 92 to 95 percent** (tuning 92%, 93%, 94%; held out 93%, 94%, 95% — run 1's tuning split is 92%, CI **0.920 to 0.927**). The two are different failures kept apart: Evidence Integrity is fabrication, whether the Problem cited exists at all; claim agreement is reading, whether the Assistance State of the Problems a claim cites says what the claim says — a difficulty claim citing Problems that took a Hint, a strength claim citing first-try correct ones, a contrastive claim ("first try when the smaller addend comes first, but a Hint when the larger does") citing one of each.
- **Detection: crossing ten was named in 2 of the 3 runs, after Session 6 in both; change unknown in 2 of 3, after Session 13 and after Session 16.** One weakness is planted per split, so a hit is 1 of 1 (100%, CI **0.207 to 1.000**) and a miss 0 of 1 (0%, CI **0.000 to 0.793**): four hits from six chances, and no sample that could tell a good detector from a lucky one. The run that missed crossing ten is the one worth reading. At Session 18 that Coach reported, supported, that make-a-ten "draws assistance most often, at roughly half its items, and the misses do not sort by structure, by which addend is larger, or by doubles" — which is the planted rate exactly, found at the Skill level: the Learner's make-a-ten ability of 0.85 less the 0.35 weakness penalty is 0.50 on a crossing item, and within 20 every make-a-ten item crosses ten. The scorer does not count that as naming the pattern, by design: a claim about a Skill is a claim about the curriculum, not a pattern in what the child does across Skills, and the whole point of the metric is to reward the second. What the Notes show is the Coach chasing sub-patterns inside the weak Skill and refuting them one by one — smaller-first against larger-first, doubles, the size of the whole — until what is left is the Skill and its rate, which is true, honestly arrived at, and scores zero.
- **False positives** (supported Hypotheses naming a weakness the Learner has not got): **4 of 39, 7 of 44, and 10 of 45** on the tuning split (10% to 22%; run 3's 10 of 45 is 22%, CI **0.125 to 0.363**), **3 of 30, 0 of 20, and 2 of 20** held out (0% to 10%). Nothing changed between those runs but the sampling: that is the width of a three-run spread on a forty-item sample, not a trend.
- **358 of 360 Plans accepted from the Coach on the first attempt, 2 after one retry, 0 Baseline fallbacks** across the three runs (120, 118, 120 first time). Not one Session of 360 fell through to the Baseline Plan.
- **Sessions to Mastery.** Mean Skills Mastered by Session 20, Coach against Baseline: **5.75, 5.75 and 6.50 against 6.00** on the four tuning Learners, **5.50, 5.50 and 5.00 against 6.00** on the two held out. Said plainly: **the Coach is not faster to Mastery than the fixed gate**, and on the held-out pair it is behind it in all three runs. The reason is in the design of both. The Coach spends Sessions testing a Hypothesis across Skills — three make-a-ten items and two unknown-addend ones to separate two explanations — while the Baseline drills one Skill until 8 of the last 10 first attempts are right and then moves to the next. Against a Simulated Learner whose ability is a fixed number, drilling wins by construction: these children cannot learn from practice, so a Session spent diagnosing is simply a Session not spent at the gate. That is a limit of the instrument as much as of the Coach, and a **practice effect in the Simulated Learners is the named next step**; until it exists, Sessions to Mastery measures how fast a planner confirms what is already true, not how fast a child learns.
- **Stories: 87 to 93 percent valid on the model's first attempt** (26, 27 and 28 of 30; run 1's 87% carries CI **0.703 to 0.947**), **97 to 100 percent valid within the three bounded attempts**, and **at most one template fallback** in any run (0, 1, 1). The rejections were Theme vocabulary and, three times over three runs, altered numbers — caught by the validator every time. **Readability 66 to 72 percent** of the valid Stories (19 of 29, 20 of 30, 21 of 29; run 3's 66% carries CI **0.473 to 0.801**), under a Judge that **agreed with the hand-labelled Calibration Set on 20 of 20 Stories in every run** (100%, CI **0.839 to 1.000**) with **kappa 1.00** against the 0.60 floor: the gate opened on its own terms rather than being waived. The Judge and the writers are both Claude models; that same-family limitation stands.
- **Summaries: 6 of 6 valid on the first attempt in every run** (100%, CI **0.610 to 1.000**), no template fallbacks, and **faithfulness 6 of 6 in every run**. The Judge agreed with the Calibration Set on **9 of 10 Summaries in run 1** (90%, CI **0.596 to 0.982**, kappa 0.80 — it failed one Summary the label passes) and **10 of 10 in runs 2 and 3** (100%, CI **0.722 to 1.000**, kappa 1.00), clearing the 0.80 threshold and the 0.60 kappa floor in all three, so the scores were reported rather than withheld.
- **Cost, measured.** **$26.27 to $27.12 a run** — $27.1220, $26.4639 and $26.2714, **$79.86 for the three** — of which the Coach is about 95 percent, at **$0.2083 to $0.2148 per Session**, so about **$0.21 a Session** averaged over the 20 Sessions across which its Notes grow. The dollars are the published rates applied to the measured tokens, not the invoice.

What the numbers are is a narrower thing than what they measure. Six hand-designed Simulated Learners, whose ability is a fixed number and who therefore cannot learn from practice; one planted weakness per split; and three runs. The gates, the validators, and Evidence Integrity are measured over tens of thousands of items and are tight. Everything that rests on the Coach's judgement — detection, false positives, Sessions to Mastery — rests on a handful, which is why it is quoted as a range across three runs with its intervals and not as a score.

**Next steps the runs point at.** A cheaper Coach: Opus 5 at high effort is measured at $0.21 a Session, Sonnet 5 at medium effort with prompt caching should land near $0.05, and the eval is the instrument that says whether the cheaper Coach still finds the planted weaknesses, read off the two reports' telemetry rather than estimated by hand. Then a practice effect in the Simulated Learners, so Sessions to Mastery measures learning and not only confirmation; the rest of Grade 1 (compare problems, three addends, place value); a Judge outside the Claude family; and a Coach that reads across Sessions rather than one at a time.

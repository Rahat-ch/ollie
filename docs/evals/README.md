# Eval Runs

Every `pnpm eval` writes one dated JSON report here, named by its UTC time (`2026-09-11T23-05-00Z.json`), and regenerates the seven charts from that file. `pnpm eval:chart` redraws them from the latest report, or from any earlier one with `--report`. Reports are committed so a number in the write-up or the video can be traced to the run that produced it. Each report says which Generation ran the Coach (`coach.generation`: `fake`, or the model id); the chart says so in its subtitle.

The charts (ticket 01 of the visuals) are drawn by code from a report file and never from a live run, in the app's paper and ink tokens, each with a title, a subtitle naming the Coach that ran and the report's date, and no number that is not in the report:

- `convergence.svg` — Skills Mastered by Session, Coach against Baseline, one facet per Simulated Learner.
- `evidence-integrity.svg` — the integrity rate as the headline, with the citations checked, the unknown Problem IDs, and the citations that disagree with their own claim, so 1.00 reads as "every citation real".
- `detection.svg` — each Learner with a planted weakness on a Session timeline, marked at the Session a supported Hypothesis first named it, or called never.
- `false-positives.svg` — per Learner, the supported Hypotheses with the false ones marked off the end, tuning and held out labelled.
- `plan-sources.svg` — Coach, Coach after a retry, and Baseline fallback over every Session the Coach planned.
- `story-validity.svg` and `summary-validity.svg` — first attempt, within the bounded attempts, and template fallbacks, then the Judge's gate: the score reported, or withheld with the agreement fraction against the Calibration Set and the threshold.

The run plays the six Simulated Learners (`src/evals/learners.ts`) for 20 Sessions each under the Coach and under the Baseline on identical seeds. The convergence section records, per Learner and per planner, the Session in which each Skill became Mastered, the first-try rate, and the share of Problems in the target accuracy band (a true first-try chance of 0.7 to 0.9). The hypotheses section scores the Coach run: for each planted weakness, the Session after which a supported Hypothesis first named it in its own words (crossing ten, missing addend), never just the Skill; false positives, supported Hypotheses naming a weakness the Learner does not have; Evidence Integrity over every citation in every Notes the Coach wrote, rejected attempts included, so an invented Problem ID counts even though the engine refused it; the cited ID must exist in the Log so far and its Assistance State must agree with the claim (a difficulty claim, "misses", "needs a Hint", cites Problems that were not first-try correct; a strength claim, "solid", "no Hints", ones that were; a refuted Hypothesis or a claim with no polarity is checked for the ID only); and how many Plans came from the Coach, from a retry, or from the Baseline fallback. The two held-out Learners, change-unknown weakness and fast fatigue, are never used to tune a prompt; every report flags them and scores the tuning and held-out splits separately. Simulated Learners only; never a real child's data.

The stories section (from ticket 10) writes one Story per Theme and Unit 3 structure, 30 in all, for a seeded sample of Problems, with the Nickname placeholder. Validity is deterministic: the share accepted by the validator on the model's first attempt, the share that ended as a model Story within the three bounded attempts, how many fell back to the template sentence, and every rejection reason with its count (numbers altered or missing, too many words, a word outside the Theme's vocabulary, and so on). Readability is the Judge's: Opus 5 with a written rubric (a six-year-old could follow it after one hearing; what happens matches the arithmetic; it reads as a scene in the Theme) over the valid Stories, but the score appears only when the Judge first agrees with the hand-labelled Calibration Set of 20 Stories (`src/evals/calibration.ts`, 12 labelled pass and 8 fail, all of which the validator accepts) on at least 80%; otherwise `judge.readability` is null and the text report says the scores were withheld. Every Story written, its rejections, and its judgement are kept in the report. On the fake, the Story writer is the template's second shape (always valid) and the Judge is the validator's opinion, which agrees with 12 of 20 labels and so fails calibration by design: a fake report shows the gate working, not the models. The Judge and the Story writer are both Claude models; that same-family limitation is stated in the write-up.

The summaries section (from ticket 12) writes the Parent Summary the app would have written after each Simulated Learner's last Coach Session, six in all, from the engine's tally of that Session Log and the Notes the Coach left. Validity is deterministic: the share the Summary validator accepts on the model's first attempt and within the two bounded attempts, how many fell back to the hand-written template, and every rejection reason with its count (a number the Session Log does not support, a claim about how the Learner was thinking, a part too long or empty). Faithfulness is the Judge's: Opus 5 with a second written rubric (every claim supported by the evidence the Summary was written from; no claim about the Learner's thinking; first-try correct, Hint-assisted, and Revealed kept apart), but the score appears only when the Judge first agrees with the hand-labelled Calibration Set of 10 Summaries (`src/evals/calibration.ts`, 6 labelled pass and 4 fail, all of which the validator accepts) on at least 80%; otherwise `judge.faithfulness` is null and the text report says the scores were withheld. On the fake, the Summary writer is the tally in plain sentences (always valid) and the Judge is the validator's opinion, which agrees with 6 of 10 verdicts and so fails calibration by design.

The first report, `2026-09-11T00-16-16Z.json`, is from ticket 05 and has the Baseline alone; the chart refuses it with a message, and its numbers are reproduced byte for byte in every later report's `convergence.baseline`.
